# 1:1 audit, 2026-10-16

Superseded for planning by `docs/AUDIT_1TO1_2026-10-18.md` (2026-10-18), whose section 0 marks which of the items
below are fully closed and where the rest went.

Where Streetball Revival still differs from the original game (PS2, `SLUS_206.51`), ranked by how much a player
sees it. State audited: `main` at f46fdbb (scorecard: 116 obligations, 77 matched, 12 known differences, 27
untested). Every item names its evidence; "measured" means numbers from the original's own captures under
`$EVIDENCE` (`street-native-ai-attract-1/2.jsonl`, computer against computer; `street-played-*.jsonl`, a person
playing), "labelled" means the rewrite's code marks it as its own or a stand-in.

How this was done: every "rewrite's own", "stand-in", "not ported" and "not traced" label in the code (about 120),
the 39 parity cases that are not matched, the handoff's open lists, and new measurements of the tip-off, the
turnovers and the pass flights in the first attract capture (40,889 updates, 55 shot phases).

The scorecard counts selected obligations, not the whole game: several of the largest differences below (the game's
phase machine, pass flights, the tip pass) had no parity case until this audit.

## 1. What a player sees first

### 1.1 The court is mirrored

The original's team 0 attacks +x (the left of the screen from the Street camera); the rewrite's team 0 attacks −x.
Camera constants are the original's, so the whole picture is mirrored: the home side attacks the other basket from
the original's, and every measurement against a capture has to mirror positions (`ai/capture.rs`,
`played_capture.rs` do). The facing-angle bug fixed today (`timing::facing_angle`) came from this, and
`timing::sector` (pass rows, catch rows, dunk and lay-up approach) is under review for the same error (session
"The sector angle in the original's frame"). Fix: flip the simulation's frame to the original's once, consistently in
the sim, the AI, the camera, the HUD's sides and the tests, so no mirroring remains anywhere.

Done (2026-10-16, branch `court-frame`): the simulation is in the original's frame (team 0 attacks +x, hoop 0 at
+36.75, `restart::side(0)` = +1), the capture tests compare positions as captured, and `timing`'s hand conversions
are gone (`docs/TRACE.md` "The court's frame"). The deterministic capture comparisons read the same numbers; the
rewrite's own computer matches play alike over 200 games; the home side attacks the left-hand basket, as the
original's. Snapshot 38.

### 1.2 The game's phase machine is inferred, not ported

The original's game keeps a phase (1 opening, 2 loose ball, 3 shot, 4 bringing it up, 5 half-court offence, 6
restart, 8 over) and changes it in its own code (`FUN_0013e4c8`, `FUN_0013ee10`, `FUN_0013d868`; the AI reads it). The
rewrite has only `Phase::Live`, `DeadBall` and `Over` and guesses the original's number each update from the ball and
positions (`ai::track`, `crates/street-sim/src/ai/mod.rs`).

Measured, first attract capture: all 8 of the original's turnovers out of phase 5 move the game to phase 4 (6) or a
restart (2); the rewrite's 6 turnovers in the same comparison stay in phase 5. The bringing-up phase is where the
original's computer plays its fast break and back-court passing: 1,036 updates of players fronting a pass in phase 4
against the rewrite's 391, and 34 back-court passes against 18. Fix: port the phase machine as a state machine
(its transitions and their conditions), drive the AI and the restarts from it, and add a parity case.

Update (2026-10-16, branch `phase-machine`): ported (`street_sim::game_phase`, `docs/TRACE.md` "The game's phase
machine", parity case `gameplay.game_phase_machine`). Of the 8 changes of possession out of phase 5, 6 are
turnovers (all to phase 4) and 2 are gamebreaker baskets (phase 5 to 6: a staged shot posts no launch); the
rewrite's turnovers now go to phase 4 too. The back-court play is unchanged by the port (the AI's bring-up).

### 1.3 Pass flights use the rewrite's own speeds

`passing::PassRelease` gives every pass a speed of 35 to 50 units a second (labelled "the rewrite's values"). The
original times a pass in its release callback (`FUN_00173570`): distance over a speed constant (`DAT_004b99c0`) with a
minimum (`DAT_004b99c4`) and a small random lob (`FUN_0014c408(0.075 + rand × 0.075)`).

Measured, 168 pass flights: no pass flies fewer than 20 updates (a third of a second); phase 4 passes take a median
20 updates over 8.8 units, phase 5 passes 29 updates over 19.6 units; long passes about 41 updates for 42 units. The
rewrite's short passes arrive in about half the original's time. Fix: trace `FUN_00173570`'s flight and lob, port it,
and add a parity case against the 168 captured flights.

### 1.4 The tip-off

- **The tip pass is a slow lob in the original.** Both attract openings: the winner's tip (`FUN_0014cb08`, then
  `FUN_0015f930(actor, 0)`) flies 40 updates at about 10.7 units a second and is caught 38 updates later. The rewrite
  throws it as an ordinary pass (`PassStyle::Normal`, 50 units a second), about 9 updates.
- **The tip height is a stand-in.** The original tips when the winner's hand bones are within 1.25 of the ball at the
  height his script lifts him to; the rewrite tips when the ball falls to 14.7 (`toss::TIP_HEIGHT`, labelled).
- **The jumpers do not play their scripts.** The original's winner rises to 15.0 (`0x457a7b`), the losers to 10.0 or
  11.0 (`0x457a12`, `0x457ae4`, by `r & 1`); the rewrite starts its generic jump for both and draws the loser's choice
  without using it.
- Matched already: the launch 118 updates after the intro, the toss's flight and spin, the contest's draws and winner
  (`presentation.opening_toss_launch`, `gameplay.ai_decisions` in part).
